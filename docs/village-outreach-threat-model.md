# Village Outreach safety design

The Flutter app authenticates through Supabase and calls FastAPI over HTTPS. FastAPI stores requests, offers, messages, and reports in PostgreSQL. Signed-in members can browse requests with rounded coordinates. The requester and accepted helper can read their conversation. The reports API accepts submissions and returns a receipt. The design below assumes a student beta with designated test accounts and a small moderation team.

| Priority | Risk | Controls and release checks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| High | A signed-in member uses repeated searches, descriptions, photos, or messages to locate someone who asked for help. | Keep exact coordinates and contact details out of browse and notification responses. Warn before posting addresses or phone numbers. Strip location metadata before making photos available. Add blocking that stops new contact in both directions and verify that no new messages or notifications reach the blocked member. |
| High | Members use public requests or private messages to solicit prohibited sales or coordinate harm. | Add reporting from posts and individual messages, retain the reporter's privacy, and give designated moderators an audited queue with suspension and review actions. Rate-limit posting, offers, messages, and repeated reports by account. Use automated signals to prioritize human review. |
| High | Someone creates misleading accounts or farms volunteer badges to gain trust. | Use verified email for account access and consider verified phone as a second contact check for ride eligibility. For an actual identity check, use a provider and store the check result and timestamp. Review any driver or vehicle eligibility separately. Keep badges private and use the existing accepted-offer and completion records. Treat unusual completion patterns as moderation signals if badges gain public visibility or rewards. |
| High | A ride location feed exposes a rider or driver to tracking after a trip ends or through an unauthorized viewer. | Prototype in Android with synthetic trips. Create a trip only for an accepted transportation request. Require both participants to opt in, show who can see the location, and allow either participant to stop sharing. Limit live access to those participants and scope each update to the active trip. Test third-account access, early stop, revoked permissions, stale GPS, and network loss. |

For ride evidence, keep start, stop, consent, and access events in an audit record. Set a short automatic deletion period for precise route points before enabling real collection. A reported trip could trigger an access-controlled hold with an expiry and a record of every access. Keep coordinates and share tokens out of application logs. The team still needs to choose who can review a reported ride, how long it can be held, and how participants will be told. Verify these decisions before testing with real people or real routes.

Volunteer recognition currently appears in My Profile. The count comes from completed requests where the signed-in member was the accepted helper. The milestones are 1, 5, and 10 completed requests. Existing assistance records contribute automatically.

Account Settings shows the email confirmation status from Supabase. Uploading a profile photo replaces the member's previous photo in the private database. FastAPI serves it to people authorized to view the member card through an offer or accepted conversation. Government ID verification needs a hosted provider. When connected, FastAPI should start the provider session for the signed-in member, verify the provider's signed result, and store the decision and verification date. Keep ID images and selfies with the provider. Test in the provider sandbox before collecting real documents. Add the ride eligibility check on the server once the team defines which ride roles require identity, driver, and vehicle checks.
